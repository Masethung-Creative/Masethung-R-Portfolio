## The problem

Five products, one designer, no shared system. The SFJ website, the Bskilling
site, an LMS, Caspa, and an ATS had each grown their own patterns. Same company,
five different-looking interfaces.

## What I did

**Found the overlap.** Audited all five for the components that already
repeated — buttons, forms, tables, navigation — and standardised those first
instead of trying to redesign everything at once.

**Protected the time.** Blocked three hours daily for product work so it
wouldn't get eaten by the 500+ monthly asset requests coming from five business
verticals. Without that boundary, product design loses to whatever is urgent.

**Kept one system.** New screens pull from the same component set, so
consistency is the default rather than something to enforce afterward.

## The constraint worth naming

Being the only designer across five products means depth is rationed. Every
hour on the LMS is an hour not on the ATS. The system approach was the response
to that — reusable decisions cost less than fresh ones.
